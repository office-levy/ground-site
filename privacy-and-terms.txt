Compile Privacy Policy
======================

Version dated 27 October 2026.

In short
--------

- Compile is a Mac app. Your library and boards live on your Macs. A board kept
  Only on this Mac is never kept in Compile’s cloud. Its pictures, while Visual search is on, and
  what you ask about it still pass through on their way to Claude.
- Compile’s cloud keeps a directory of accounts, carries the boards you share, and passes what you
  ask of Claude to Anthropic.
- A private board is sealed from its first share, and its room in Compile’s cloud has no key to the
  sealed part. What it held before its first share goes unsealed, and Compile’s cloud can read that
  part. Compile asks first.
- PDFs are the exception. A PDF on a board that isn’t kept Only on this Mac is kept in Compile’s
  cloud: its file sealed on a private board, and the text your Mac read from it where Compile’s
  cloud can read it, so it can be searched. That text isn’t end-to-end encrypted.
- A public board is public: anyone can see it, in Compile and on the web.
- Compile shows no ads and sells no data. It includes Sparkle for updates. It counts on your Mac
  what is done in Compile. It sends the counts once a day, with nothing that says who or which Mac
  sent them. You can turn this off.
- The Feed suggests public boards, pictures and people beyond those you follow, learns from what
  you do with its suggestions, and says why it suggests each.
- With an account, Compile also learns from what you do in it, linked to your account: which of its
  own commands and steps you use, and when. Never your pictures, boards or what you type. Claude
  sorts what you write to Compile into themes. You can turn this off in Settings ▸ Privacy.

Who is responsible
------------------

Compile’s operator runs Compile, and is the controller responsible for the processing this policy
describes. For privacy questions or requests, while signed in, choose Write to Compile… in the
Account menu. That note is linked to your account and includes your Compile and macOS versions.

What an account needs
---------------------

To make an account, Compile needs a username and a sign-in with Apple or Google (with Google, your
email address too). It also needs your Mac’s public key and certificate, and, while Compile is by
invitation, an invitation code. You also confirm you are 16 or older. Without these, Compile can’t
make an account. Anything else you give, such as your name or a picture, is up to you.

Without an account you can still use Compile on your Mac: your library, boards kept Only on this Mac,
search, and image recognition on your Mac. Sharing boards, following people, the Feed and Claude need an
account.

On your Mac
-----------

Your library, your boards and Compile’s search index start on your Mac. Compile’s on-device models
(what kind of picture it is, the words in a screenshot, what it shows) run on your Mac and send
nothing.

Compile adds the boards you belong to, private ones too, to your Mac’s Spotlight index, by name and
people. Your own boards and public ones also go in with their words and a preview. Public boards you
only visit are left out. Anyone using your Mac may find these in Spotlight. Compile doesn’t send this
index to its cloud.

Copying or dragging puts pictures on your Mac’s clipboard (or drag pasteboard). Compile’s own formats
there, for board items and pictures, can be read by other apps. They name your files by a token that
only the same run of Compile can read back, not by where they are. Other apps get a copied picture as
a copy of its file without its metadata, and can see where that copy is. Compile doesn’t send the
clipboard to its cloud. macOS’s clipboard features and other apps follow their own rules.

Compile fetches a link’s title and picture from the web for cards you or people you follow placed, or
when you choose Load Preview. For cards kept in Compile’s cloud, only the Mac of whoever placed
the card asks the web page; everyone else draws its picture and icon from Compile’s cloud.
The site sees the fetching Mac’s IP address. When you bring in an Are.na
channel by its link, your Mac reads it from Are.na and downloads its pictures from where they are
kept. Those sites see your IP address too. If your Boards folder or library is in Dropbox or iCloud
Drive, that service carries those files under its own terms.

What leaves your Mac, and who keeps it
--------------------------------------

Compile’s directory (accounts)

What it gets:
- Your username, and the private ID Apple or Google gives Compile (your name isn’t asked for).
- If you sign in with Google, the email address it has verified for you, which other people on
  Compile never see. Apple isn’t asked for an email.
- Your public key and its fingerprint, which tell other Macs who you are.
- If you give them, your full name and profile picture, and an Instagram handle (only an earlier
  version of Compile asked for one).
- Your choices of who may find you, see your name, and see whom you follow and who follows you.
- For each of your Macs: its certificate (without your name) and its name in System Settings. Also
  when it was added and last seen, and whether it signed out or you removed it.
- The ids of the boards you are on, not their names or contents, so your other Macs find them. Also
  which boards’ rooms you founded, which count toward your account’s rooms and storage.
- Whom you follow, who follows you, requests to follow and whom you block, unsealed so the directory
  can act on them.
- Letters to you: an invitation to a board, or a note about your public work. The note says someone
  placed pictures you found on a public board of theirs, or forked a public board of yours. They are
  sealed for your Mac, and the directory keeps no record of who sent them.
- Reports you send, and feedback and requests you send with Compile’s and macOS’s versions. Your Mac
  sorts each note into feedback or a request first, with Apple’s on-device model if it has it.
- Your plan, Stripe’s ids for you, and your credits and limits.
- A record of each use of Claude: when, what kind, which model, how large and what it cost, but not
  what was asked.
- Counts that enforce limits, naming your account or your network’s IP address.
- What you did with the Feed’s suggestions, what it showed you, and your own ranker for them.

How long: until you delete your account, except:
- counts: 2 days
- what you did with the Feed’s suggestions: 180 days; what it showed you: 14 days
- a letter: until your Mac fetches it, 30 days at most
- a request to follow that nobody answers: 90 days
- each use of Claude: 40 days
- a username you give up: held a month for nobody else, with nothing attached

Feedback and requests are kept for 12 months, or until you delete your account if sooner.

Invitation codes. While Compile is by invitation, the directory keeps each code: whose it is to
give, when it was made, and when it made an account. Until a code is used, its web page tells
Compile’s cloud when it is opened, and whether on a Mac; the directory keeps the first and the last
time. For a code Compile’s operator sends to someone it invites from a list, it also keeps which
account the code made. Your own codes are deleted with your account.

Each board’s room

Every board not kept Only on this Mac has a room in Compile’s cloud. It carries the board between its
people’s Macs, and holds it for a Mac that was offline.

What it gets:
- The board’s history, signed by its people’s Macs. On a private board it is sealed from the first
  share, except which Mac made each change, and when.
- The certificates of the people on it, with the names they gave Compile.
- The keys that let Macs in, not those that open what is sealed.
- Pictures, so they can be fetched while whoever placed them is offline, sealed on a private board.
- On a board whose PDFs are kept in Compile’s cloud, an index of the words of its PDFs, readable by
  the room, so they can be searched.
- Each of its Macs’ builds and when it last heard from them.
- An invitation link’s mailbox and preview (a picture of the board and its people’s names). These
  are sealed with a key from the link, which the room doesn’t hold.
- On a public board: all of it unsealed, and a picture of the board for its page. Also, for each
  network that read it, a hash of its IP address. Earlier days’ hashes are deleted when someone
  first reads the board on a later day; until then they stay. The hash uses no secret, so an IPv4
  address could be worked out from it.

How long: while anyone is on the board. An invitation’s preview is deleted when its link is used
up, expires or is closed. When a public board goes private, its room shuts out everyone not on it
and removes its pictures. It also lets go of its PDFs’ open files, which its people’s Macs put up again sealed. Ninety days after the last person on a board throws it away, its room is
due for deletion. If its removal from Compile’s search is still pending, the room waits for that
first. What someone placed stays in the board’s history after they leave, for the people on it.

PDFs on shared boards

When you place a PDF on a board that isn’t kept Only on this Mac, your Mac reads it, every page,
scanned pages by text recognition on your Mac. It sends Compile’s cloud the file as it is, sealed
with the board’s key on a private board and unsealed on a public one, and a picture of its first
page the same way. It also sends the text it read, in passages with their page and lines and the
PDF’s title. Compile’s cloud keeps the file, picture and text in Cloudflare R2, where it can read the
text: it isn’t end-to-end encrypted. A PDF’s text and vectors are kept separately for each board
and supplied by the Mac that places it. Only people on the board can reach any of it,
and on a public board anyone who can see it. Compile’s directory keeps which boards hold what, its
size, and which account sent it, for storage limits and Compile’s costs.

When you ask about a PDF’s visual contents, your Mac can download and open its file, then send
pictures of the needed pages to Claude while Visual search is on. The verified file stays in the same
cache as a PDF you open yourself.

How long: while a board holds the PDF. Taking it off a board removes that board’s text and
vectors. Its file and picture are deleted when no board holds them. Text kept under the previous
shared format remains only while an existing placement still holds it. A board thrown away lets go of them after its 90 days. An upload left part way is deleted
after a week. A PDF someone opened stays in a cache on their Mac until Compile or macOS clears it,
and the picture of its first page stays with their copy of the board.

Pictures and cards’ pictures

On a board that isn’t kept Only on this Mac, Compile’s cloud keeps the lasting copy of its pictures
as well as its PDFs. Each picture goes up as the copy that may leave your Mac (without where, when
or with what it was taken), sealed with the board’s key on a private board, as it goes to the
board’s room today, under a name that says nothing of which picture it is. The picture and icon of
a link card go up the same way, from the Mac of whoever placed the card, and everyone else on the
board draws them from Compile’s cloud: their Macs no longer ask the web page, so the page sees only
the person who placed the card. A picture you put on such a board from anywhere but your library (a
file from the Finder or Photos, Add to Compile, a picture dragged from the web, a clipped page, one
brought from Are.na or Instagram) is no longer copied into your library folder: it waits in Compile’s
own folder until the cloud holds it, then in a cache that clears itself. The file you placed it from
is only read, never moved, changed or removed, and pictures from your own library stay in your
folder. A Mac puts up only what its person placed. When a board’s storage is full, its window says
what didn’t go up, and nothing more is sent.

- On your Mac. Pictures you receive, and cards’ pictures from Compile’s cloud, are kept in your
  Mac’s caches folder, which macOS may empty and which isn’t backed up. Compile lets go of those shown
  longest ago, except boards, PDFs and pictures you make available offline. That choice stays on
  this Mac, in its preferences, and is never sent.
- What Compile’s cloud keeps. The sealed copies in Cloudflare R2 while a board holds them, and
  their names. On a public board, a picture anyone may see goes open and is named by its hash, as
  today. When the board’s room has moved its pictures to the store, it empties what it kept.
- Builds. Your Mac tells the board’s room, and Compile’s cloud when it asks for its settings,
  which build of Compile it runs. The cloud counts builds by day, with nothing that names you or your
  Mac, to know when no Mac still needs the old way pictures were kept. The room keeps each of its
  Macs’ builds and when it last heard from them.

Compile’s search across all of Compile

- Public boards are listed in Compile’s search by default. A board’s people can turn that off.
- For a public board listed in search, it also keeps the words of its PDFs, with each PDF’s title
  and page, so search can find words inside them. They leave with the board or the PDF.
- Pictures marked Hide from Search are never sent to it.
- What you type is sent to Compile’s cloud to answer each search, and isn’t stored.
- Compile’s cloud keeps each listed board’s name, id and maker. For each picture, it keeps the id,
  caption, a small copy, numbers for matching look-alikes and size. It also keeps what Claude read
  in it, and where it came from as the board’s signed history shows. These make public pictures
  searchable, and connect copies of a picture.
- When a board goes private, is taken down or thrown away, or its people turn listing off, Compile
  asks search to remove it. If search can’t be reached, the removal is retried every minute until
  confirmed, even after a room restarts. Until then the board may still show in search: removal
  isn’t promised to be instant. A small copy is deleted when no listed board and no upload still
  needs it. Copies people already made stay theirs.

Your files in Compile’s cloud

When you sign in, Compile adds a place called Compile to Finder’s sidebar, beside Dropbox, and macOS
asks you to switch it on. Nothing goes there unless you put it there. Files you keep there are
sealed on your Macs before they leave. Compile’s cloud keeps the sealed bytes (in Cloudflare R2),
their size, when they changed and an id; their names and what is in them stay sealed. A file you
delete leaves the cloud at once, and its record after 90 days. Signing out takes the place away
from that Mac, keeping anything not yet sent.

The find log (not turned on)

When this is on, your Mac sends a 32-byte code for each day it takes in pictures, which commits to
them. Without your key it says nothing about which pictures, or whose. The log is public and
permanent. The directory counts what each account sends, for 2 days.

Anthropic, for Claude

Only when you use it:
- Visual search: each picture, reduced to 1,024 pixels, and its file’s name. Hidden pictures are
  never sent. Turn it off in Settings ▸ Library. When it is off, or Claude can’t be reached, a Mac
  with Apple’s on-device model analyzes pictures locally, and that sends Anthropic nothing.
- Asking in ⌘K: what you ask, the board, and your library’s words for what you ask and for the
  selected pictures. When your words may mean them, also the names of your boards (up to 200) and an
  overview of your library. Background readings need Plus; reading pictures on demand during an ask
  is included in that ask’s budget. While Visual search is on, Claude may also see everything on the
  board, whoever placed it: a picture of the board as laid out with each item marked, small pictures
  of what is on it, a few pictures chosen for what you asked, sharper, and any picture or PDF page it
  asks to look at. It may read the PDFs on the board that are on your Mac, or kept in Compile’s cloud for the board: how each one begins (up to
  200 words), the passages that fit what you asked (up to sixteen, or twenty-four when you name a PDF or ask where something is said), and up to six pages at a time when
  it asks for them. When the board’s PDFs on your Mac are not too long (about sixty pages of text, or thirty scanned),
  each goes whole instead: every page’s
  text, and a picture of each scanned page. Never a file itself. What earlier answers inferred about
  a picture (who likely made it, when, where) is kept on your Mac, never backed up, and goes with later
  asks about its board as an inference, never as a record; a picture you captioned carries none. With the board goes when each thing on it was placed, and
  who placed it, named as “you” or “Person A”, never by name.
- Reading: while Visual search is on, Claude reads once each picture you place on a board someone
  else is on, or a public one. This lets search find it by what it shows. It goes shrunk to 1,024
  pixels, with nothing of its name, captions or board. The reading is kept on your Mac, out of its
  backups. It is also kept, for as long as the board is, in the room of each board you placed the
  picture on. There it is sealed on a private board, and unsealed on a public one. A reading made
  overnight waits in Compile’s cloud until your Mac collects it, a week at most.
- Library passes: while Visual search is on, your Mac also sends Claude some of your library’s
  words now and then. File names, 50 at a time, to name the buildings and architects in them. Your
  library’s commonest tags, to group words that mean the same. And at night, up to 200 file names
  with their captions and keywords, to suggest boards. Hidden pictures’ file names are never sent.

When you use visual search or ask, Compile sends Anthropic no account id, username or name you gave Compile.
But the pictures an ask tells Claude about may carry the name of the person who found them, and the
site they came from. And Compile’s cloud may send Claude an account’s username and full name, with
the name, Are.na page and website of someone invited from a list. This checks whether the account is
theirs, and Compile’s operator confirms or rejects every such match. On a board other people are on, the board’s conversation keeps what you asked and what Claude
answered, in the board’s history: sealed on a private board, unsealed on a public one.

Boards connected to Compile’s cloud also keep a shared memory of questions, supported answers and
follow-up questions contributed by their members. This helps answers arrive sooner and suggests
questions about the board. This memory is encrypted with the board’s key, including on public
boards, and available only to current members. Compile’s cloud keeps up to 100 entries per board for
30 days; your Mac keeps an encrypted copy. Older board versions can help locate evidence, but
aren’t replayed as current answers. Clearing your conversation removes your contributions from
this cloud memory and the cached copy on that Mac; it does not erase other members’ contributions
or copies they already received. Boards kept Only on this Mac do not contribute.

A description made overnight waits in Compile’s cloud until your Mac collects it, a week at most.

Anthropic’s published API policy is to delete what it is sent, and its answers, normally within 30
days. There are exceptions for particular services, agreed terms, safety enforcement and legal
requirements, so this is no promise that nothing is retained. Pictures sent overnight as one batch,
with what Claude wrote of them, are kept by Anthropic up to 29 days. During an ask, Anthropic keeps
the part each turn repeats for a few minutes, for your ask alone, to answer its next turn sooner.
Its commercial terms forbid training models on its API customers’ content. See Anthropic’s API
retention
policy (https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-my-organization-s-data)
and Commercial Terms (https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms).

Compile’s operator can look up an account by username or account ID. Its operator page shows the
account’s plan, cloud storage, internal allowances, request counts and AI costs. Only the operator
can see these detailed usage metrics; they are not shown in customer Settings, account menus or
standard account exports.

To see how well picture search works, Compile’s cloud counts descriptions and readings across everyone:
whether each was made, and if not, why. It also counts which of an architect, a project, a place and
a year each description named, and how many keywords it gave. These are words and numbers, never the
picture, what Claude said of it, or whose it was. Compile’s operator sees the totals. It may also
look over pictures on public boards, which anyone can see, with their captions and what readers
agree they show.

Stripe and billing

You pay on Stripe’s own pages. Stripe gets your card details and whatever its pages ask for, and
Compile sends it only your account’s id. Compile keeps Stripe’s ids for you, your plan’s state and
your credits. It reads your card’s brand, last four digits and expiry, and your invoices, from
Stripe, to show them. Deleting your account first deletes you as Stripe’s customer. Plus ends at
once, without a refund of the time left in the period you paid for, and Stripe forgets your card.
Stripe may keep transaction records after your account closes, to meet its legal duties; deleting
your Compile account doesn’t erase them. See Stripe’s retention
policy (https://support.stripe.com/questions/data-retention-policy).


Sparkle, for app updates

The Developer ID version of Compile includes Sparkle. When update checks are set up, it reads
Compile’s update feed and downloads updates from Compile’s cloud, which sees your IP address. Each
check says which build of Compile is asking. Compile’s cloud counts checks by build and by which of
its web addresses was asked, with nothing about who or which network asked.

Apple and Google

Signing in with Apple or Google gives Compile a private ID for you. Compile asks Apple for no name and
no email. It asks Google for your email address too, but not your name. Compile keeps the address if
Google has verified it for your account, so Compile’s operator knows whose account it is. Your export
shows it; other people on Compile, your profile and Compile’s web pages never do. It is updated when
you next sign in with Google after it changes there, and is deleted with your account. Compile also
uses the address, and any Instagram handle you gave, to recognise someone Compile’s operator invited
from a list who joined another way. Google learns that you signed in to Compile, as Apple does.

Your recovery code is kept in your iCloud Keychain, which Apple syncs to your devices, and deleting
your account removes it.

Anonymous counts (Improving Compile)

Compile counts on your Mac how it is used, to learn where people get stuck and what to improve. Your
pictures, their names, your boards and what you type or ask are no part of it.

What a day’s report holds:
- How often each of Compile’s own commands and steps was used (a board made, a search that found
  nothing, an ask put back). Also which followed which.
- How far a few tasks got (finding, asking, Grow, sharing, signing in), and how each ended.
- How long a few things took, in coarse steps, and how many crashes and hangs macOS reported.
- Where clicks land on each kind of window, in a coarse grid, with no time and nothing about what
  was clicked.
- How far boards are zoomed, and how many cards are in view.
- What kinds of thing were placed (a photograph, a screenshot, a YouTube link), and from where
  (Finder, search, a paste).
- Which parts of Compile the day was spent in, and whether it was a brief, steady or long day.
- With Share the kinds of things you collect on: broad subjects (an interior, lighting, a plan)
  for what was placed, searched for and not found. They come from a fixed list of about 60, and are
  worked out on your Mac. A search’s words are matched there and dropped, never kept or sent.
- Three facts about the Mac’s use, and no others. Compile’s version (not the build). Whether this is
  its first week, first month or later. Whether Compile was used on 8 or more of the last 28 days.

Every word in a report comes from a list built into Compile, and Compile’s cloud refuses any report
with a word outside it. Settings ▸ Privacy ▸ Improving Compile ▸ Show… shows today’s report and the
last one sent, byte for byte as they go.

Census answers. Apart from the day’s report, your Mac answers a few single facts. Each goes
alone, with no counts and at its own random time, so none can be joined to a report or another
answer. Each fact goes at most once a week, and the week and month answers once in each. The facts
are:
- the number of pictures in the library (in five broad steps), and of boards (in four)
- whether the Mac’s language is English, whether it is signed in, and whether visual search is on
- the macOS major version
- that it was used this week, and this month

How it leaves:
- Unsent reports wait on your Mac for up to 14 days, then are deleted.
- One report a day, each alone, at a random moment in the 24 hours after its day ends. Each is
  unsigned, with no cookie, no account, nothing that names the Mac, and a browser name that is the
  same for everyone.
- Compile’s cloud sees your IP address, as with every request. It holds it in memory only, to allow
  at most 24 reports a day from one network. It is never written down, and the request isn’t logged.
- Reports wait in Compile’s cloud under random keys, with no time of arrival. Once a night they are
  written in a shuffled order, under new random keys. They are kept 400 days. Cloudflare’s 30-day
  restore points of the store where they wait can show when a report waited, but never where it came
  from.

Who reads them: no one reads a single report, only summaries. These leave out anything made from
fewer than 5 Macs’ reports of the same day. Compile’s operator sees them in Compile. Claude
(Anthropic) may read them to write Compile’s operator a short brief, and sees totals only.

Your choices:
- Share anonymous counts and Share the kinds of things you collect, in Settings ▸ Privacy ▸
  Improving Compile, are on until you turn them off. Turning either off deletes at once what it
  hasn’t sent.
- You accept this policy, counts included, when you make your account. Without an account nothing is
  sent: what is counted stays on your Mac and is deleted after 14 days.

Reports can’t be exported or deleted on request, because nothing in them says whose they are.

What Compile learns from what you do

With an account, Compile also learns from what you do in it, linked to your account, to see what helps
and what gets in the way, and to make Compile better for you and for everyone. It is on for every
account until you turn it off.

What it keeps:
- Each time you use Compile on a Mac: when it began and ended, how many things you did, and Compile’s
  version.
- For each day: which of Compile’s own commands and steps you used, how many times, and the first and
  last time. These are the same words as the anonymous counts above (a board made, a picture placed
  from Finder, a search that found nothing, an ask put back), from the same list built into Compile.
- None of it is your pictures, their names, your boards or anything you type or ask.

Your Mac sends each of these once it ends, signed as your account’s other requests are. Unsent ones
wait on your Mac for up to 14 days. Signing out deletes what waits.

What it is joined with: the records about your account this policy already describes: when you
joined, and through which invitation if Compile’s operator invited you; your plan and what you paid;
what Compile’s Claude cost for you; what you did with the Feed’s suggestions; and what you wrote to
Compile. From these Compile’s operator sees, for each account, its weeks in Compile, and what it earns
and costs Compile.

What you write to Compile: the words of Write to Compile…, the details of a report you send, a
reply to an invitation, and notes Compile’s operator keeps from speaking with you. Compile’s cloud
sends the words to Claude (Anthropic), twice and separately, to sort them into a fixed list of
themes (such as “search finds the wrong thing”), each with the words that show it. Claude is sent
the words, never your account. Compile’s operator sees the themes and the words, linked to your
account.

Who sees it: Compile’s operator, in Compile. The operator’s people lists and account pages show
your username and account ID alongside the activity kept about your account, your plan and costs.
The operator can open the same account page by username or account ID. Other customers cannot see
these pages. Claude reads totals, the themes and these facts to write the operator a weekly brief,
and is sent no account ID for that brief.

How long: what you did, 400 days; the themes of what you wrote, a year. Then it is deleted. It
is deleted with your account, and your account’s export includes it.

Your choice: Share what I do, in Settings ▸ Privacy, is on until you turn it
off. Turning it off is your objection to it: Compile stops at once, deletes what your Mac hasn’t sent,
and deletes what its cloud kept of what you did. Turning it on again starts afresh. What you write
to Compile is still read so Compile’s operator can answer it. Turning activity sharing off does not
remove the billing, storage and operational usage records needed to run your account. The operator
can still look up those records by username or account ID; the activity timeline is absent.

The Feed’s suggestions

Besides what people you follow make public, the Feed suggests public boards, public pictures and
people you might like, from everyone’s public work. It suggests only what is public, and a person
only if their own choices in Settings ▸ Privacy let you find them.

What your Mac sends: when the Feed asks for suggestions, up to four short summaries of what your
boards are about. Compile’s model makes them on your Mac, as numbers, not pictures, names or words.
Compile’s cloud uses them to answer, and keeps none of them.

What Compile’s cloud keeps, with your account:
- What you did with each suggestion, and when: that you were shown it, opened it, placed from it or
  made your own copy. Or that you asked for more like it, followed someone through it, or chose Not
  Interested. Kept 180 days.
- What the Feed showed you, and how it described each one to its ranking. Kept 14 days.
- Your own ranker: numbers learned from what you did, which decide what the Feed suggests to you.
  Kept until you delete your account.

Nobody else sees these. What everyone does with suggestions helps choose them, without saying who
did what. The Feed suggests what people who acted on the same things as you also acted on, and what
the people you follow follow. It also suggests what people place together on public boards. Compile’s
operator sees only totals: how many things were done, and how many rankers there are. Settings ▸
Privacy ▸ Export My Data… includes all three, and deleting your account deletes them.

Your choices: Not Interested takes a suggestion away, and blocking or muting someone takes them

Cloudflare

Cloudflare runs Compile’s cloud (the directory, the rooms, R2, Vectorize, Workers AI, the web pages), so every request
passes through it, with your IP address. Compile turns off Cloudflare’s log of each request. Compile’s
cloud does write its own messages, such as errors, to Cloudflare’s Workers Logs. They are written to
name nobody, and Cloudflare keeps them for a few days, as its plan sets: 3 on Workers Free, 7 on
Workers Paid. Cloudflare keeps restore points of the directory and the rooms for up to 30 days.

Workers AI makes, from the text of PDFs on shared and public boards and from the words
searched there, what finds passages by meaning. Cloudflare says it does not use that content
to train AI models. For these inference requests, it keeps inputs and outputs only as needed

keeps each board’s text and vectors in Cloudflare’s storage as described above; Cloudflare
may also retain operational metadata for running, securing and billing the service.

GitHub, for the directory’s backup

Compile’s operator keeps offline. GitHub keeps it for 90 days, so accounts can be put back if
